Built for the upgrade grind

Every EHR upgrade drops hundreds of changes on clinical operations at once. Release Reaper separates what matters from the noise before your teams ever see it.

Complete extraction

Upload a release-notes document and every distinct change is pulled out as its own structured entry — nothing buried in prose, nothing counted twice.

Categorized & domain-tagged

Each entry is classified — new feature, enhancement, bug fix, workflow change — and tagged to the EMR domains it touches, from orders and results to scheduling and billing.

Impact scoring

Entries are scored for operational impact and flagged for what needs action: training, build changes, policy review, or end-user communication.

Safety signals surface first

Changes that affect patient safety or clinical data rise to the top of the brief — not lost on page forty of a vendor PDF.

Version-to-version compare

Fuzzy comparison across releases shows what is genuinely new, what changed, and what carried over — so upgrade planning starts from a diff, not a reread.

Exports your team already uses

Take the results out in six formats — including JSON, CSV, and Markdown — ready for ticket systems, training plans, and leadership summaries.

How it works

Four steps between a vendor document and a brief your CMIO will actually read.

1

Upload

Drop in the release notes — PDF, Word, text, Markdown, or HTML, from any EMR vendor.

2

Analyze

Entries are extracted, deduplicated, categorized, domain-tagged, and impact-scored automatically.

3

Review the brief

The Clinical Ops Brief leads with action-required items: safety, workflow, and training impacts first.

4

Export & assign

Send the results to the tools your teams work in, with owners and next steps attached.

The Clinical Ops Brief

One document per release that answers the only questions leadership asks about an upgrade:

  • What is changing that affects patient care or clinical data?
  • Which workflows, roles, and departments are touched?
  • What needs training, build work, or a policy decision — and by when?
  • What can safely be ignored this cycle?
